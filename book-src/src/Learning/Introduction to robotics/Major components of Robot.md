## MAJOR COMPONENTS OF ROBOT

Robots consist of several key components that collectively enable their functionality.

**1. Sensors:**
<div style="text-align:center;">
    <img src="../../Assets/Images/major component -sensor.jpg" width="250">
</div>
- Robots heavily rely on an array of sensors to capture crucial environmental data, ensuring they can navigate and interact effectively.
- These sensors come in various types, including cameras for vision, ultrasonic and infrared sensors for distance measurements, and accelerometers and gyroscopes for tracking orientation and motion.
- Cameras, for instance, not only provide vision but also enable object recognition, which is, essential for tasks like sorting and inspection.
- Additionally, the integration of advanced sensors such as LiDAR and radar further enhances a robot's ability to perceive and understand its surroundings, contributing to improved navigation in complex environments.

**2. Actuators:**
- Actuators play a pivotal role in translating decisions made by the robot's control system into physical actions.
- Beyond standard motors and servos, robotic arms and grippers add versatility to a robot's capabilities.
- Robotic arms equipped with end-effectors allow for intricate tasks, while adaptive grippers enhance the robot's ability to manipulate diverse objects, showcasing the adaptability and flexibility of robotic systems.

**3. Controller and control system:**
<div style="text-align:center;">
    <img src="../../Assets/Images/major component - control system.png" width="250">
</div>

- The controller and control system serve as the central nervous system of the robot, managing the coordination between sensors and actuators.
- Incorporating powerful microcontrollers and sophisticated control algorithms enables robots to execute complex tasks with precision.
- Real-time processing capabilities and machine learning algorithms within the control system enhance the robot's ability to adapt and learn from its experiences, contributing to continuous improvement.

**4. Intelligence:**
<div style="text-align:center;">
    <img src="../../Assets/Images/major components - intellegence.png" width="250">
</div>

- The intelligence of a robot extends beyond basic decision-making to encompass learning and adaptation.
- Through machine learning algorithms, robots can refine their actions over time based on accumulated data.
- This adaptive intelligence allows robots to autonomously optimize their performance, making them well-suited for dynamic environments where tasks may evolve or change.

**5. Power Supply:**
<div style="text-align:center;">
    <img src="../../Assets/Images/major components - power supply.jpg" width="250">
</div>

- The choice of power supply significantly impacts a robot's performance and operational capabilities.
- In addition to traditional batteries and power cables, emerging technologies like energy harvesting mechanisms, such as piezoelectric systems, can contribute to sustainability by converting ambient energy into power.
- This dual focus on efficiency and environmental impact reflects advancements in power sources for modern robotic applications.

**6. Communication:**
<div style="text-align:center;">
    <img src="../../Assets/Images/major components - communication.jpg" width="250">
</div>

- Effective communication is essential for collaborative robotics and seamless integration into interconnected systems.
- Beyond conventional communication methods like Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, emerging protocols such as 5G contribute to low-latency and high-bandwidth connections, enabling robots to transmit and receive data rapidly.
- This interconnectedness facilitates not only human-robot communication but also communication between multiple robots, fostering collaborative efforts in various industries.
