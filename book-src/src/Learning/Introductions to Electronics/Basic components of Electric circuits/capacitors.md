When a capacitor is present in a circuit, it can store electric charge, and the amount of charge stored is determined by the formula

`Q=C.V`
Q: Electric charge stored (in coulombs, C)

C: Capacitance of the capacitor (in farads, F)

V: Voltage across the capacitor (in volts, V)

The equation Q = CV shows that the charge stored is directly proportional to the capacitance (C) and the voltage (V) applied to the capacitor.This property plays a crucial role in numerous electronic applications.

**Types of Capacitors**
- There are various types of capacitors, each with distinct properties.

- Ceramic capacitors are small and economical, making them suitable for general electronics. 
- Electrolytic capacitors offer high capacitance and are polarised and used in power supplies. 
- Tantalum capacitors are stable and compact and are found in precision applications. 
- Film capacitors are stable and versatile. 
- Polymer capacitors provide reliability. 
- Supercapacitors store significant energy for rapid release. 
- Variable capacitors have adjustable capacitance for tuning circuits.
